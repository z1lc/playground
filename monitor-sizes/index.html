How to read it. Makers quote a curved screen's active area and diagonal along the panel surface (pixel pitch × pixel count), so the front view draws every screen as if flattened. The top view turns that arc back into desk geometry: the straight-line width between the screen's edges (the chord) and how far the edges reach toward you from the centre (the curve depth), with the maker's chassis width and depth without stand underneath. A radius like 1000R means every point of the screen is 1000 mm from one spot — hover a curved screen to see where that spot is. The side-by-side pairs are drawn toed in, meeting at the seam, with each screen aimed so its outer edge lands where a curved ultrawide’s does (the 32-inch pair at the Neo G9’s, the 27-inch pair at the LG 52’s). Physical area is the active screen surface — unrolled width × height for curved screens, and the two panels without the seam for the side-by-side pair — so a 47.6-inch 16:9 TV has more screen than a 51.6-inch 21:9 ultrawide. PPI is computed as 25.4 mm ÷ pixel pitch. Screens are centred on one another, as they would be at eye level.
