# Georgia Ballot 2026: research dossier

Background research behind the ballot guide in `index.html`, for Fulton County voters in Georgia's November 3, 2026 general election. It covers the statewide, congressional and countywide contests, plus the countywide school sales-tax question on the back. Researched October 12, 2026. The guide itself is neutral and makes no recommendations; this dossier carries the fuller context, sourcing caveats and gaps that didn't fit on the page.

Sourcing order: candidates' own campaign sites, debate transcripts and questionnaires; official sources (Secretary of State, General Assembly, FEC, State Ethics Commission, Fulton County); then Georgia news outlets reporting first-hand. Ballotpedia and Wikipedia appear only as a last resort and are flagged as secondary sources in the guide. Betting-market odds come from `build_markets.py`.

# U.S. Senate and Governor

## U.S. Senate: Mike Collins (R) vs. Jon Ossoff (D, incumbent)

**How they got here.** Ossoff ran unopposed in the May 19 Democratic primary, winning 1,042,331 votes ([GA SOS](https://results.sos.ga.gov/results/public/Georgia/elections/GeneralPrimary51926)). On the Republican side, Rep. Mike Collins led the May 19 primary with 40.5%, ahead of Derek Dooley (30.2%) and Rep. Buddy Carter (25.1%). He then beat Dooley 55.5% to 44.5% in the June 16 runoff ([GA SOS runoff results](https://results.sos.ga.gov/results/public/Georgia/elections/06162026GeneralPrimaryRunoff)). President Trump endorsed Collins on June 14, two days before the runoff. Gov. Brian Kemp had backed Dooley ([Atlanta News First](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2026/06/17/us-rep-mike-collins-defeats-derek-dooley-georgia-senate-gop-runoff/)). Kemp endorsed Collins on Sept. 14, saying "the future of our state and country demands a different direction" ([CBS Atlanta](https://www.cbsnews.com/atlanta/news/gov-brian-kemp-endorses-collins-in-u-s-senate-bid-to-unseat-ossoff-a-different-direction/)). Senate Majority Leader John Thune campaigned with Collins in Atlanta on Sept. 25. He said the Senate Leadership Fund would keep spending in Georgia despite polling that shows a widening gap ([GPB](https://www.gpb.org/news/2026/09/25/senate-majority-leader-john-thune-campaigns-for-mike-collins)). Former GOP Sen. Jeff Flake endorsed Ossoff on Aug. 4, saying "character matters" ([AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/08/former-gop-sen-jeff-flake-backs-jon-ossoff-saying-character-matters/)).

**Ballot and runoff.** The Libertarian Party could not gather the roughly 72,000 signatures needed, so no Libertarian is on the statewide ballot ([AJC, Jul 11](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/07/libertarians-wont-be-on-the-ballot-in-georgia-meaning-likely-no-runoffs/)). Write-in votes could still, in theory, force a Dec. 1 runoff in a very close race. Allen Buckley, the Libertarian nominee, appeared as an option in Trafalgar's September poll (1.3%), but he is not on the printed ballot ([Trafalgar, Sep](https://www.thetrafalgargroup.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/GA-General-Full-Report-0912.pdf)).

**Debates.** An Oct. 8 debate took place, hosted by Atlanta News First rather than the Atlanta Press Club. Rick Folbaum moderated at Assembly Studios ([AJC recap](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/10/georgia-us-senate-race-jon-ossoff-and-mike-collins-debate/)). Coverage called it caustic and personal. Ossoff opened by calling it "the trial of Mike Collins" ([CBS Atlanta](https://www.cbsnews.com/atlanta/news/ossoff-and-collins-trade-personal-attacks-during-heated-georgia-senate-debate/)). Collins called Ossoff "a trust fund kid who's never had a job in his life" and "a radical" ([AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/10/georgia-us-senate-race-jon-ossoff-and-mike-collins-debate/)). A full transcript is posted by [WTOC](https://www.wtoc.com/2026/10/09/read-full-transcript-us-senate-debate-between-jon-ossoff-mike-collins/). Policy points from that debate:
- **Tariffs and costs.** Collins said "the tariffs now are making it fair" and "tax cuts are the answer." Ossoff said "These tariffs are crushing our state" and that diesel is up 70% since Trump was elected.
- **Health care.** Collins said "The Affordable Care Act is what has caused your insurance to go sky high." He said the 2025 law put money into rural hospitals and praised "Trump RX." Ossoff pledged to "repeal the disastrous Medicaid cuts" and said premiums "have doubled for more than a million Georgians."
- **Immigration.** Collins said "You should come into this country legally, period" and "we need H-2A visas to be fixed." Ossoff said "Georgians do expect and deserve secure borders" and criticized raids on job sites "with no suspicion of any criminal history."
- **Data centers.** Both favored local control. Collins said "These data centers need to pay." Ossoff said "no Georgia family or small business should pay a dime more" to subsidize data-center energy use.
- **Surveillance cameras.** Both raised concerns about Flock license-plate cameras. Collins said police should need "a warrant to access that information."

The second debate, in the Atlanta Press Club Loudermilk-Young series, is **Tuesday, Oct. 13, 8–9 p.m.**, live on GPB ([APC schedule](https://atlantapressclub.org/debates/); [GPB](https://www.gpb.org/news/2026/09/22/statewide-candidates-debate-at-atlanta-press-club-minus-the-top-of-the-gop-ticket)). It had not happened when this research was done. Ossoff's campaign issued a preview statement on Oct. 12 ([Ossoff campaign](https://electjon.com/ossoff-statement-ahead-of-georgia-u-s-senate-debate-hosted-by-the-atlanta-press-club/)). Advance in-person voting begins Oct. 13.

**Polls.** Every public general-election poll since late June shows Ossoff ahead. The guide lists 12 polls with fieldwork from Jun 23 to Oct 9. Notes on how they were run:
- **YouGov** (Sep 15–18, [PDF](https://d3nkl3psvxxpe9.cloudfront.net/documents/ttw_ga_20260915.pdf)) is recorded at its registered-voter number (51–41). Among strict likely voters (n=3,071) it was 51–43.
- **Big Data Poll** (Sep 21–23, [release](https://www.bigdatapoll.com/blog/peach-state-poll-jon-ossoff-opens-up-lead-over-mike-collins-in-georgia/)) had registered voters at 51.0–40.1 and likely voters with leaners at 55.4–44.6.
- **Quantus** (Sep 14–16) went from 48.2–44.4 on the initial ballot to 49.0–44.9 with leaners ([ANF](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2026/09/18/poll-ossoff-holds-4-point-lead-over-collins-tight-georgia-senate-race/)).
- **Wedgewood** (Oct 2–3) asked a two-way forced choice, so its numbers have no undecided share.
- **Fox News** (Jun 23–27) and **Wick** (Jun 27–30) were fielded in June but released in July. Both are included and labeled.
- The **AARP** poll (Jul 13–16) found Collins ahead among voters 50 and older, 53% to 45% ([AARP PDF](https://www.aarp.org/content/dam/aarp/research/topics/voter-opinion-research/politics/2026-midterm-election-poll-georgia.doi.10.26419-2fres.01065.004.pdf)).

Cook Political Report and Sabato's Crystal Ball rate the race Likely Democrat ([CBS Atlanta](https://www.cbsnews.com/atlanta/news/ossoff-and-collins-trade-personal-attacks-during-heated-georgia-senate-debate/)).

**Money.** No Q3 2026 FEC reports were on file as of Oct. 12. The October Quarterly is due Oct. 15, and the latest filings are July Quarterly reports covering through June 30.
- **Ossoff:** $97,986,263 in receipts over the 2021–2026 cycle, $59.7M spent, and $42,587,451 cash on hand as of June 30 ([FEC](https://www.fec.gov/data/candidate/S8GA00180/?cycle=2026&election_full=true)). His campaign said it raised about $20M in Q2 from more than 474,000 donations, averaging $42 ([AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/07/jon-ossoff-enters-fall-campaign-with-42m-on-hand/)).
- **Collins:** $7,409,349 raised (Jan 2025–Jun 30, 2026), $2,079,981 cash on hand across his committees, and $461,916 in debts ([FEC](https://www.fec.gov/data/candidate/S6GA00390/?cycle=2026&election_full=true)). His campaign reported about $2.1M raised in Q2 from almost 16,000 contributors ([AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/07/jon-ossoff-enters-fall-campaign-with-42m-on-hand/)).
- **Outside money:** The Republican-aligned Senate Leadership Fund pledged an initial $44M to the race (same AJC article).

**Stock trading and voter ID.** Collins voted yes when the House passed the Stop Insider Trading Act, H.R. 7008, 232–198 on July 22 ([House roll call 280](https://clerk.house.gov/evs/2026/roll280.xml)). The bill bars members and their families from buying individual stocks. It lets them keep existing holdings, requires 7–14 days' notice before a sale, and adds a photo-ID requirement for voting ([NOTUS](https://washingtonsun.com/money/congressional-stock-ban-bill-passes-house)). On Sept. 30 a motion to begin debate in the Senate failed 53–47 on a party-line vote. Ossoff voted no ([Senate roll call 253](https://www.senate.gov/legislative/LIS/roll_call_votes/vote1192/vote_119_2_00253.xml)). Democrats called the voter-ID language a "poison pill," and Schumer said "this bill does not ban stock trading" ([Daily Caller](https://dailycaller.com/2026/09/30/stop-insider-trading-act-senate-vote/)). Ossoff's campaign says he leads efforts to ban congressional stock trading ([bio](https://electjon.com/bio/)). He did not appear to issue a statement explaining this vote.

**Abortion.** Collins was an original cosponsor of the 2023 Life at Conception Act ([Congress.gov, H.R. 431](https://www.congress.gov/bill/118th-congress/house-bill/431/cosponsors)). POLITICO, as summarized by PRRI (a weak, secondhand source), reported that Collins called himself "100 percent pro-life" in 2022. It also reported that on the 2026 campaign trail he said he supports "Georgia's heartbeat law, which includes exceptions" ([PRRI](https://prri.org/election-update/collins-abortion-stance-georgia/)). The POLITICO original could not be retrieved. Ossoff's campaign says he defends "freedom of choice in reproductive health care" ([bio](https://electjon.com/bio/)).

**Controversies and fact-checks.**
- **House Ethics review (Collins).** On Jan. 5, 2026, the House Ethics Committee said it would further review an Office of Congressional Conduct referral about Collins. It noted this "does not itself indicate that any violation has occurred" ([Ethics Committee](https://ethics.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Press-Release-Rep.-Mike-Collins-1.5.26.pdf)). The referral alleges he used official funds to benefit former chief of staff Brandon Phillips. That includes about $5,000 paid to Phillips's girlfriend as an intern, who the referral says did no work. A Collins spokesman called the complaint "a sad attempt to derail" him. His lawyer said the intern did provide services ([AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/01/ethics-committee-reviewing-claims-us-rep-mike-collins-misused-funds/)). No final finding has been reported.
- **Extremism allegations (Collins).** Ossoff cites a May Slate report that former Collins chief of staff Kip Talley was in a group chat with white nationalists Nick Fuentes and Richard Spencer. He also cites social-media posts by Collins's son-in-law. Collins called the allegations "craziness" and said "I've condemned racism, antisemitism, hate. I don't care where the source is" ([CBS Atlanta](https://www.cbsnews.com/atlanta/news/ossoff-and-collins-trade-personal-attacks-during-heated-georgia-senate-debate/); [AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/10/georgia-us-senate-race-jon-ossoff-and-mike-collins-debate/)).
- **2020 election (Collins).** He has said on the campaign trail that Trump won the 2020 election. He did not directly answer the question at the debate ([AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/10/georgia-us-senate-race-jon-ossoff-and-mike-collins-debate/)).
- **Social Security ad (Ossoff).** PolitiFact rated **Mostly False** an Ossoff ad saying Collins wants to push older Americans off Social Security "back to work." The ad relied on a partial quote from a 2025 radio interview about tax policy ([PolitiFact](https://politifact.com/factchecks/2026/sep/15/jon-ossoff/social-security-georgia-mike-collins-retirement/)).
- **Debate fact-checks.** PolitiFact said Ossoff's "$70 billion to data center developers" claim "needs context," since the law has no data-center-specific provisions. It rated inaccurate Collins's claim that Ossoff voted for the Green New Deal ([PolitiFact](https://politifact.com/article/2026/oct/09/collins-ossoff-georgia-senate-debate-data-centers/)).
- **Israel (Ossoff).** He voted in December to block some U.S. arms transfers to Israel. He said he concluded Israeli forces violated humanitarian law, and declined to call Gaza a genocide, calling that "a question for the International Criminal Court." He said he would "make no apology for my votes." Collins says Ossoff "talks one way and he votes another way" ([CBS Atlanta](https://www.cbsnews.com/atlanta/news/ossoff-and-collins-trade-personal-attacks-during-heated-georgia-senate-debate/); [AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/10/georgia-us-senate-race-jon-ossoff-and-mike-collins-debate/)).

**Gaps.** Collins's campaign site has no issues page; his positions come from his House and campaign bios, debate remarks and news. Ossoff's campaign site also has no issues page, so his positions come from his bio page and debate remarks. Neither campaign site states an immigration or Iran position. Those rows rely on debate remarks.

## Governor: Rick Jackson (R) vs. Keisha Lance Bottoms (D), open seat

**How they got here.** Health-care executive Rick Jackson entered the Republican race in February 2026 with $50M of his own money, including a $40M ad buy ([ANF via Fox Carolina](https://www.foxcarolina.com/2026/06/17/georgia-gop-runoff-shocker-rick-jackson-beats-burt-jones-despite-trump-kemp-backing/)). He finished second in the May 19 primary (32.5%), behind Lt. Gov. Burt Jones (38.4%). Secretary of State Brad Raffensperger got 15.0% and Attorney General Chris Carr 11.9% ([GA SOS](https://results.sos.ga.gov/results/public/Georgia/elections/GeneralPrimary51926)). Jackson then beat Jones 52.6% to 47.4% in the June 16 runoff ([GA SOS runoff](https://results.sos.ga.gov/results/public/Georgia/elections/06162026GeneralPrimaryRunoff)), even though Trump and Kemp both backed Jones ([ANF via Fox Carolina](https://www.foxcarolina.com/2026/06/17/georgia-gop-runoff-shocker-rick-jackson-beats-burt-jones-despite-trump-kemp-backing/)). About 48 hours later, Kemp and former Govs. Nathan Deal and Sonny Perdue endorsed Jackson ([WTOC](https://www.wtoc.com/2026/06/18/runoff-shockwave-rick-jackson-lands-kemp-deal-perdue-triple-endorsement/)). On July 22, at a Trump Accounts event in Cobb County, Trump said he was "now supporting" Jackson. Jackson pledged to have Georgia add money to children's Trump Accounts, and said he would pay for it himself if lawmakers did not ([ANF](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2026/07/23/trump-promotes-trump-accounts-georgia-rally-weighs-statewide-races/)). Keisha Lance Bottoms won the Democratic primary outright with 56.2% against six rivals, including Jason Esteves (18.7%), Mike Thurmond (12.9%) and Geoff Duncan (7.0%) ([GA SOS](https://results.sos.ga.gov/results/public/Georgia/elections/GeneralPrimary51926)). Obama endorsed her by video on Oct. 8 ([FOX 5](https://www.fox5atlanta.com/news/obama-endorses-keisha-lance-bottoms-georgia-governor-race)).

**Debates.** The Oct. 6 Atlanta News First town hall, moderated by Rick Folbaum, was the only joint debate ([ANF highlights](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2026/10/07/grocery-tax-flock-cameras-biggest-moments-georgia-governor-debate/); [AJC takeaways](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/10/takeaways-from-georgia-governor-debate/)). Jackson formally declined the Atlanta Press Club debate set for Oct. 12 at 8 p.m. on GPB, so Bottoms was scheduled to answer questions next to an empty podium. The Jackson campaign said Bottoms "cannot reach Georgians without Rick's help" ([GPB](https://www.gpb.org/news/2026/09/22/statewide-candidates-debate-at-atlanta-press-club-minus-the-top-of-the-gop-ticket)). Jackson also skipped the Atlanta Press Club's June 1 runoff debate to campaign in Kennesaw ([GPB](https://www.gpb.org/news/2026/06/02/lt-gov-burt-jones-debates-empty-podium-rick-jackson-campaigns-in-kennesaw)). Highlights from Oct. 6 ([Georgia Recorder via Cobb County Courier](https://cobbcountycourier.com/?p=113098) unless noted):
- **Medicaid.** Jackson said "I am going to expand Medicaid to cover everybody" while keeping work requirements ([AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/10/takeaways-from-georgia-governor-debate/)). He also said expansion alone "doesn't solve the cost." Bottoms said "My opponent has made money off of this broken healthcare system."
- **Income tax.** Jackson pledged to cut the income tax in half in his first term "or I won't run again."
- **Data centers.** Bottoms wants "a pause on all new construction until we create a path forward that best serves all of Georgia."
- **Trump and Kemp.** Bottoms said Jackson wants to be "Trump's favorite governor" and added, "I want to be yours, Georgia." Jackson said "I will stand up to anybody, just like Brian Kemp has done."
- **Pay.** Bottoms pledged to raise the minimum wage and wants a $60,000 starting salary for teachers ([ANF](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2026/10/07/grocery-tax-flock-cameras-biggest-moments-georgia-governor-debate/)).
- **Elections.** Jackson praised Kemp's election laws and said no Justice Department investigation is needed. Bottoms supports the John Lewis Voting Rights Act and an Election Day holiday ([ANF](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2026/10/07/grocery-tax-flock-cameras-biggest-moments-georgia-governor-debate/)).

**Polls.** Five polls since mid-September show Jackson ahead by 1–3 points, all within their margins of error:
- Rasmussen, Sep 14: 48–45 ([Rasmussen](https://www.rasmussenreports.com/public_content/politics/elections/election_2026/election_2026_georgia_governor))
- YouGov, Sep 15–18: 45–44 among registered voters, 47–44 among strict likely voters ([PDF](https://d3nkl3psvxxpe9.cloudfront.net/documents/ttw_ga_20260915.pdf))
- Big Data Poll, Sep 21–23: 48.7–46.7 among likely voters, 50.5–49.5 with leaners
- InsiderAdvantage, Sep 22–23: 48–46
- Wedgewood, Oct 2–3: 51–49, forced choice

Summer polls leaned the other way or were tied: State Navigate (Jul 10–13) Bottoms 51–44 with leaners, AARP (Jul 13–16) Bottoms 48–46, Quantus (Jul 29–Aug 1, sponsored by the Natural Gas Association of Georgia) Bottoms 46.4–44.9, and InsiderAdvantage (Aug 16–17) 46–46. Some caveats:
- State Navigate said its sample ran about 8 points bluer than the presidential electorate. Reweighted, Bottoms led by 4 ([State Navigate PDF](https://statenavigate.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/GA_Statewide_July_StateNav_Briefing_PartyID-1.pdf)).
- Trafalgar's two fall Georgia surveys asked only about the Senate race.
- No October polls other than Wedgewood were found for this race.

**Money.** Georgia's 2026 schedule has no Sept. 30 report. The latest reports cover through **July 31**, and the next is due **Oct. 20** ([GA Ethics Commission schedule](https://ethics.ga.gov/filing-schedule/)). PeachFile, the commission's 2026 filing system, requires a login, so these figures come from news reports of the filings.
- **Jackson's campaign:** raised $6.1M from June 11 to July 31 and had $4.3M on hand. It has raised over $118M in total, including self-funding, with nearly all of it spent. It took in no new cash from Jackson during the period, only in-kind items such as legal fees and rent. His leadership committee, For All Georgians Inc., gave the campaign $5M ([Capitol Beat](https://capitol-beat.org/2026/08/jackson-leads-bottoms-in-fundraising-for-georgia-governors-race/)).
- **Jackson, all accounts:** the AJC counted about $13M raised in the period, including a $10M Republican Governors Association contribution to his leadership committee, and about $11M on hand across accounts ([AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/08/georgia-governor-race-rick-jackson-raises-13m-in-outside-money/)).
- **Jackson's self-funding:** he gave his campaign more than $111M of his own money and spent more than $108M by early July, about $83M of it on TV and cable ads ([AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/07/heres-how-rick-jackson-spent-108-million-in-the-republican-race-for-governor/)). His campaign then announced an $87M general-election ad buy ([ANF](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2026/07/31/rick-jackson-campaign-launches-first-ad-massive-87m-buy/)).
- **Bottoms's campaign:** raised $3.1M from May through July 31 and had just over $770,000 on hand. It has raised $5.7M since May 2025. She filed a day late ([Capitol Beat](https://capitol-beat.org/2026/08/jackson-leads-bottoms-in-fundraising-for-georgia-governors-race/)).
- **Bottoms, all accounts:** the AJC counted more than $5.6M including over $1.6M from the Democratic Governors Association, more than $3M raised by her leadership committee since June, and about $1.3M on hand by her campaign's account ([AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/08/georgia-governor-race-rick-jackson-raises-13m-in-outside-money/)).

**Controversies, with each side's response.**
- **State contracts (Jackson).** Jackson Healthcare subsidiaries have received more than $1B in state payments since a 2020 no-bid contract. Jackson pledged to end state contracting by Dec. 31 if elected ([FOX 5 profile](https://www.fox5atlanta.com/news/rick-jackson-georgia-governor-candidate-profile)).
- **Abortion recordings (Jackson).** In June, the AJC and HuffPost reported April audio in which Jackson appeared to agree with an attendee that rape victims should have to prove rape to qualify for an exception. He also said a pregnancy from rape is "still a life." His campaign called this "Democratic spin." It said he "strongly supports Georgia's current Heartbeat law" and its exceptions ([CBS Atlanta](https://www.cbsnews.com/atlanta/news/audio-recordings-reveal-georgia-gop-gubernatorial-candidate-rick-jacksons-views-on-abortion-exceptions/)).
- **Immigration ad (Jackson).** A primary ad using the phrase "deported or departed" drew backlash. Jackson later said his focus is on people who commit crimes ([FOX 5 profile](https://www.fox5atlanta.com/news/rick-jackson-georgia-governor-candidate-profile)).
- **Data centers (debate dispute).** Bottoms says Jackson invests in data centers, and Jackson denies owning or investing in any ([ANF](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2026/10/07/grocery-tax-flock-cameras-biggest-moments-georgia-governor-debate/)). Jackson says Bottoms signed off on Atlanta data centers that received tax relief. She says her only approval was a non-hyperscale facility at Georgia Tech ([Georgia Recorder via Cobb County Courier](https://cobbcountycourier.com/?p=113098)).
- **National Guard (debate dispute).** Bottoms said Jackson was recorded saying the National Guard would be called in for the election. Jackson replied, "I've never suggested that we have the National Guard to come in on this election. Period" ([AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/10/takeaways-from-georgia-governor-debate/)).
- **Mayoral spending (Bottoms).** Jackson's first general-election ad cites city money spent on her husband's Super Bowl flights and an alleged $11,000 in car-service costs. Her campaign says she recognized the purchases were not made on a personal account and the money was repaid. Her spokesperson called Jackson "Slick Rick" ([ANF](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2026/07/31/rick-jackson-campaign-launches-first-ad-massive-87m-buy/)).
- **Atlanta crime (Bottoms).** An AJC analysis found reported homicides in Atlanta rose 59% from 2019 to 2020, compared with 33% in other cities examined, while robberies and reported rapes fell. Experts caution against attributing pandemic-era crime to a single mayor, and Bottoms disputes Jackson's portrayal ([FOX 5 profile](https://www.fox5atlanta.com/news/keisha-lance-bottoms-georgia-governor-candidate-profile)).
- **ICE jail contract (Bottoms).** As mayor in 2018 she ended the city's jail contract with ICE over family separations, a decision she still cites ([AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/04/keisha-lance-bottoms-a-look-at-the-ajcs-coverage/)).

**Other positions not shown in the guide.**
- **Jackson:**
  - Gas tax: automatically suspend it for 60 days after a price spike, defined as a 35-cent rise in 7 days or 75 cents in 30 days.
  - Foster care: halve adoption times.
  - Anti-trafficking: use AI and data tools against trafficking.
  - Veterans: create a Governor's Office of Military and Veterans Affairs.
  - Medicaid: in August he favored work requirements with a fixed federal block grant ([FOX 5 profile](https://www.fox5atlanta.com/news/rick-jackson-georgia-governor-candidate-profile)).
- **Bottoms:**
  - Insurance and gas: end the 2.25% tax on auto-insurance premiums and lower the gas tax to 28 cents.
  - Elections: abolish the State Election Board.
  - Pre-K funding: let voters decide on casino gambling to fund pre-K.
  - Voter registration: accept verified student IDs ([FOX 5 profile](https://www.fox5atlanta.com/news/keisha-lance-bottoms-georgia-governor-candidate-profile)).
  - Taxes: she calls GOP efforts to end property taxes "irresponsible" ([AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/04/keisha-lance-bottoms-a-look-at-the-ajcs-coverage/)).

**Gaps.** Jackson has no stated position on gun policy beyond public safety, and no stated position on vouchers beyond the federal scholarship tax credit. Bottoms has no stated position on vouchers. Coverage of the Oct. 12 Atlanta Press Club governor's event was not yet available.

# Lieutenant Governor, Secretary of State and Attorney General

All three are open seats with no incumbent on the ballot. Each has exactly two candidates, one Republican and one Democrat, with no Libertarian or independent. That matches the official [Augusta-Richmond composite sample ballot](https://www.augustaga.gov/DocumentCenter/View/21864/Composite-Sample-Ballot-Nov-2026), whose statewide contests are the same everywhere in Georgia: Dolezal (R) / McLaurin (D), Fleming (R) / Reynolds (D), Strickland (R) / Miller (D), in that order.

## Lieutenant Governor

**Why it's open.** Lt. Gov. Burt Jones ran for governor and lost the GOP nomination to Rick Jackson ([Atlanta News First](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2026/09/24/kemp-backs-gop-nominee-greg-dolezal-race-georgia-lieutenant-governor/)).

**What the job really controls.** The lieutenant governor presides over the 56-member Senate. Under Senate rules, the office also controls committee assignments, bill referrals and the daily calendar ([WRDW, Sep 15](https://www.wrdw.com/2026/09/15/how-split-georgia-election-could-render-next-lieutenant-governor-powerless/)). Those powers exist only at the Senate majority's pleasure. Outgoing Lt. Gov. Burt Jones told the AJC: "The power is all dependent upon the majority caucus allowing you to have it."

The [AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/08/georgia-has-a-powerful-lieutenant-governor-will-it-stay-that-way/) recounts two times the majority stripped those powers:
- **2003:** the new GOP Senate removed Democrat Mark Taylor's power to make committee assignments.
- **2011:** the Senate stripped Republican Casey Cagle's assignment powers during a feud.

Senate Majority Leader Jason Anavitarte would not say whether the GOP caucus would do the same to a Democratic lieutenant governor. McLaurin acknowledges that risk. He said at launch that even a sidelined lieutenant governor could change "the tone of the Senate's business" ([AP via National Newswatch](https://nationalnewswatch.com/2025/05/05/democrat-josh-mclaurin-announces-run-for-georgia-lieutenant-governor-in-2026)).

**How they got here.**
- **Dolezal** finished second in the seven-way May 19 GOP primary with 23.1%, behind former Senate President Pro Tem John F. Kennedy (27.3%). The rest of the field: Blake Tillery 19.1%, David Clark 15.8%, Steve Gooch 11.7%, and two others. Dolezal then won the runoff 54.2%–45.8%.
  - One [Atlanta News First runoff story](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2026/06/17/state-sen-greg-dolezal-wins-gop-lieutenant-governors-runoff/) says Dolezal finished *first* in May. The certified SOS results show he finished second.
- **McLaurin** led the Democratic primary with 41.4%, ahead of Nabilah Parkes (39.5%) and Richard N. Wright (19.1%). He won the runoff 54.9%–45.1%. Parkes entered late, citing what she called "Islamophobic campaign ads" from Dolezal ([ANF](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2026/06/17/josh-mclaurin-wins-democratic-lieutenant-governors-runoff/)).
- In the runoff debate, Dolezal criticized his own party as having been "the party of crony capitalism." Parkes attacked McLaurin as a Democrat who "coddles" Republicans. McLaurin answered, "I get stuff done" ([Capitol Beat, Jun 1](https://capitol-beat.org/2026/06/lieutenant-governor-hopefuls-sharpen-personal-attacks-in-debate-about-leading-georgia-senate/)).

AP describes Dolezal as having led that effort; the [Georgia Recorder](https://cobbcountycourier.com/2026/04/candidates-vying-to-be-georgias-next-top-lawyer-sharpen-attacks-as-early-voting-starts/) reports that Sen. Bill Cowsert chaired the committee.
- Sponsored [SB 463 (2026)](https://www.legis.ga.gov/legislation/72849), the 500-home cap on corporate ownership of single-family homes. It passed the Senate 49–3 and stalled in the House.
- Sponsored [SR 838 (2026)](https://www.legis.ga.gov/legislation/73520), a "SAVE" constitutional amendment on citizen-only voting and photo ID. It got 32–23, short of the two-thirds needed.

## Secretary of State

**Why it's open.** Brad Raffensperger ran for governor and lost ([WRDW](https://www.wrdw.com/2026/06/17/tim-fleming-wins-gop-secretary-state-runoff/)). Republicans have held the office since 2007.

**The voting-machine question is the central issue.**
- A 2024 law, [SB 189](https://www.legis.ga.gov/legislation/64471), required counting the human-readable text on ballots rather than QR codes after July 1, 2026. The legislature never funded a replacement.
- Kemp called a special session after the June runoff. It passed [SB 3 (2026 Ex. Sess.)](https://www.legis.ga.gov/legislation/74767), which Kemp signed June 25. The law:
  - creates an Election Equipment Specifications and Standards Committee to set requirements for a new uniform voting system;
  - sets how far the Secretary of State may go in selecting, certifying or procuring that system;
  - pushes SB 189's effective date back. The [Georgia Recorder](https://roughdraftatlanta.com/?p=362611) reports QR-code counting is allowed until 2028, with added post-election audits.
- Fleming added a last-minute amendment directing the committee to focus on hand-marked paper ballot systems, with ballot-on-demand printing as the preferred method. He said, "This is just setting the parameters around what this committee will look at." Rep. Saira Draper (D) called it "a disservice because it narrows the scope."
- Fleming chaired the 2025 House study committee on the voting system. In February it recommended moving to hand-marked ballots and hand-counting any QR-coded ballots for official results ([Capitol Beat via Rough Draft](https://roughdraftatlanta.com/2026/02/03/hand-marked-paper-ballots/)).
- The [AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/10/georgia-secretary-of-state-2026/) frames the race as deciding who certifies the system Georgia uses in 2028. It notes the state spent about $107 million on the current Dominion touchscreens in 2019.

**Roll-call record on election bills.**
- **Fleming** voted for SB 189 (2024) and for SB 3 (2026). He sponsored [HB 1207 (2024)](https://www.legis.ga.gov/legislation/66838), letting candidates proof their ballot listings (signed), and [HB 1467 (2026)](https://www.legis.ga.gov/legislation/73702), making motor-voter registration opt-in (did not advance).
- **Reynolds** has no legislative record.

**How they got here.**
- **Fleming** led the five-way GOP primary with 39.2%, ahead of Vernon Jones 27.3%, Kelvin King 15.9%, Gabriel Sterling 11.9% and Ted Metz 5.6%. He beat Jones 64.5%–35.5% in the runoff.
  - WRDW noted Fleming's "irregularities" phrasing about 2020 has become coded language for Republicans who neither endorse nor reject Trump's claims. Jones said he stands "with those who believe there was election fraud."
  - Fleming skipped the April 28 GOP primary debate ([Georgia Recorder via The Current](https://thecurrentga.org/2026/05/01/georgia-sos-candidates-debate-election-integrity-2020-election-claims/)).
- **Reynolds** led the Democratic primary with 42.3%, ahead of Dana Barrett 35.2%, Cam Ashling 12.3% and Adrian Consonery Jr. 10.1%. She beat Barrett 63.1%–36.9% in the runoff.
  - Barrett questioned Reynolds' 2008 resignation from the State Court of Fulton County. Reynolds called that "political hyperbole."
  - All four Democrats largely agreed on speeding license processing and adding securities-division investigators (same source).

**Background details.**
- **Fleming:** AJC reports he was Kemp's deputy secretary of state and then Kemp's first chief of staff as governor. A 2015 data breach while he was deputy exposed personal data of about 6 million voters. An internal investigation blamed an IT employee, who was fired. Reynolds argues his tenure "doesn't mean you have a record of accomplishment" ([AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/10/georgia-secretary-of-state-2026/)).
- **Reynolds:** former Fulton County judge, star of "Family Court with Judge Penny" (2008–09), licensed mediator and arbitrator. She served in the Biden USDA as deputy assistant secretary for civil rights ([WRDW](https://www.wrdw.com/2026/06/17/tim-fleming-wins-gop-secretary-state-runoff/)). On national TV she said Trump's election moves are "a pretext" to interfere ([CBS Atlanta, Jul 21](https://www.cbsnews.com/atlanta/news/georgia-democratic-secretary-of-state-candidate-penny-brown-reynolds-accuses-trump-of-sowing-election-doubt/)).

**Other context from the AJC piece.**
- The Trump administration sued Georgia for voters' personal data. A federal judge blocked the request shortly before the AJC's Oct. 6 update.
- The Justice Department is investigating Fulton County's 2020 election, including a January raid that seized ballots.
- The State Election Board did not come up in the AJC piece. Reynolds' SEB comments come from [Capitol Beat](https://capitol-beat.org/2026/05/georgia-secretary-of-state-candidates-split-over-integrity-of-2020-election-results/). I found no public Fleming statement on the board.

## Attorney General

**Why it's open.** Two-term AG Chris Carr ran for governor ([ANF](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2026/05/20/former-state-lawmaker-brian-strickland-wins-gop-attorney-general-primary/)).

**What the office does.** The Department of Law's [duties page](https://law.georgia.gov/about-us/duties) says the AG:
- represents the state in all civil cases and in capital-felony appeals;
- prosecutes public-corruption cases involving dealings with the state;
- may bring civil or criminal actions at the governor's request;
- issues opinions that bind state agencies.

Its [about page](https://law.georgia.gov/about-us) lists consumer protection, human trafficking, gangs, elder abuse and Medicaid fraud among its key issues, and names a Gang Prosecution Unit and a Human Trafficking Prosecution Unit.

**How they got here.** Both won outright on May 19, so neither had a runoff.
- **Strickland** beat fellow state Sen. Bill Cowsert 71.6%–28.4%. Cowsert had chaired the special committee investigating DA Fani Willis. The two clashed at the April 28 Atlanta Press Club debate over the prosecution of Trump allies ([Georgia Recorder via Cobb Courier](https://cobbcountycourier.com/2026/04/candidates-vying-to-be-georgias-next-top-lawyer-sharpen-attacks-as-early-voting-starts/)).
- **Miller** beat former House Minority Leader Bob Trammell 84.6%–15.4%. In that debate, Trammell called the six-week ban an "abomination." Miller focused on corporate ownership of metro Atlanta homes, saying landlords "are using AI to collude to keep prices artificially high" ([WRDW](https://wrdw.com/2026/04/29/georgia-candidates-attorney-secretary-state-face-off-debate-stage/)).

**Legislative records (legis.ga.gov).**

# Public Service Commission and the statewide commissioners

## Public Service Commissioner, District 3

**What the PSC is.** The [Public Service Commission](https://psc.ga.gov/about-the-psc/) has five members elected statewide to staggered six-year terms. It has "exclusive power to decide what are fair and reasonable rates" for regulated utilities, chiefly Georgia Power, plus natural gas and telecom. Candidates [must live in their district, but the whole state votes](https://www.fox5atlanta.com/news/georgia-public-service-commission-candidates-4-candidates-ballot). As of October 2026 the [commissioners](https://psc.ga.gov/about-the-psc/) are:
- Republicans: Chairman Jason Shaw, Lauren "Bubba" McDonald, and Stan Wise ([appointed to District 5 in August 2026](https://gov.georgia.gov/node/23491))
- Democrats: Peter Hubbard and Alicia Johnson

That makes the commission 3–2 Republican. Democrats would need **both** District 3 and District 5 to gain a 3–2 majority. If Republicans win either seat, they keep the majority; if they win both, it becomes 4–1.

**Why there was a 2025 election and why Hubbard's term is short.** A federal lawsuit argued that electing commissioners statewide diluted Black voters' power. It [postponed the 2022 and 2024 PSC elections](https://roughdraftatlanta.com/2025/10/09/solar-power-advocate-hubbard/) before ultimately failing. After that, the [legislature set a new schedule](https://valdostadailytimes.com/2026/03/09/five-republicans-four-democrats-qualify-for-psc-elections/) that resumed elections in 2025.
- District 3 had been held since 2021 by Fitz Johnson, a Kemp appointee who had never faced voters. The 2025 contest therefore filled the seat for only [one year](https://roughdraftatlanta.com/2025/07/07/georgia-public-service-commission-election/), and the seat returns to the ballot in 2026 for a full six-year term.
- In the 2025 Democratic primary, Keisha Waites led Hubbard 46% to 33%. Hubbard then [won the July 15 runoff](https://www.wuga.org/local-news/2025-07-16/hubbard-claims-victory-over-waites-in-democratic-runoff-for-georgia-public-service-commission), and Waites endorsed him.
- On Nov. 4, 2025, Hubbard (District 3) and Alicia Johnson (District 2, over Tim Echols) [won the first Democratic statewide constitutional-office victories since 2006](https://thecurrentga.org/2025/11/04/democrats-flip-two-seats-on-the-georgia-public-service-commission/). The defeated incumbents had voted for six rate increases in about two years, which that report estimated at roughly $500 a year for an average household. Hubbard's final share was [nearly 63%](https://www.fox5atlanta.com/news/georgia-public-service-commission-candidates-4-candidates-ballot), and he took office in January 2026.

**Georgia Power decisions that frame the race.**
- **Vogtle (Dec. 19, 2023).** All-Republican commissioners voted 5–0, with Fitz Johnson then a member, to let Georgia Power [recover $7.56 billion in remaining Plant Vogtle costs](https://www.gpb.org/node/1037096). The vote added about $8.95 a month to a typical bill on top of a $5.42 increase when Unit 3 came online. In 2025, Hubbard argued that Vogtle's overruns [make new nuclear unaffordable](https://roughdraftatlanta.com/2025/10/09/solar-power-advocate-hubbard/) compared with solar and storage.
- **Large-load rule (Jan. 23, 2025).** A unanimous [rule for new customers above 100 MW](https://thecurrentga.org/2025/01/24/georgia-psc-passes-rule-for-data-centers-power-usage/) lets Georgia Power price data centers by project risk and charge them transmission and distribution costs as construction proceeds. It also requires their contracts to be filed with the PSC.
- **Base-rate freeze (July 1, 2025).** The PSC [froze base rates through 2028](https://www.wsbtv.com/news/local/georgia-psc-approves-plan-freeze-georgia-power-base-power-rates/JXNC3CLU7BAIRGFGBPUFR3K2CA/). The freeze does not cover fuel costs or storm costs such as Hurricane Helene, which are handled separately. Georgia Power projected 8,200 MW of new demand by 2031, about 80% from data centers.
- **About 10,000 MW certified (Dec. 19, 2025).** In a lame-duck 5–0 vote, the PSC [certified nearly 10,000 MW of new gas plants, battery storage and some solar](https://www.ajc.com/business/2026/02/georgia-regulators-wont-revisit-approval-of-new-power-plants-for-data-centers/).
  - Georgia Power pledged $8.50 a month of "downward pressure" on residential bills, for 2029–2031 only.
  - [FOX 5](https://fox5atlanta.com/news/georgia-psc-declines-rethink-georgia-power-expansion-data-centers) reported a projected cost of $15.6 billion; the AJC cites estimates of up to $60 billion.
  - On Feb. 18, 2026, the PSC voted 3–2 along party lines not to reconsider. Hubbard's motion to decertify gas units, including the McIntosh unit, also failed.
- **Fuel and Helene costs (May 28, 2026).** A unanimous settlement paired [lower fuel charges with Helene storm-cost recovery](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2026/05/28/lower-rates-approval-give-georgia-power-customers-50-savings-year/). The net effect is about $4 a month less for a 1,000-kWh customer, or $285 million a year in total. The Helene revenue requirement was cut from $270 million to $109 million.
  - Hubbard said the Republican majority rejected his amendments on coal purchasing and storm costs: "We missed opportunities to save Georgia families another $50 million" ([The Current](https://thecurrentga.org/2026/06/01/commission-approves-georgia-power-fuel-rate-decrease/)).
- **Large-customer pricing probe (July 7, 2026).** The PSC [unanimously opened an investigation](https://roughdraftatlanta.com/2026/07/08/georgia-power-data-center-costs/) into whether the pricing structure used mainly by data centers shifts costs to households. PSC staff warned it could raise average residential bills by up to 11% by 2028. The Republican majority rejected Hubbard's broader scope, and the probe is due by year-end.
- **OpenAI contract and Savannah meeting.** The PSC approved Georgia Power's contract to serve an OpenAI data center in Effingham County. At a [September meeting in Savannah](https://grist.org/energy/in-savannah-georgia-psc-faced-public-wrath-over-data-centers/), held at Alicia Johnson's urging, speakers pressed for more transparency. Chairman Shaw said the PSC must "do a better job of forcing the company" to submit less-redacted filings.

**Primary.** Hubbard was the only Democrat to qualify ([Rough Draft guide](https://roughdraftatlanta.com/election-guide-public-service-commissioner-dist-3-and-5/)). Johnson beat purchasing manager Brandon Martin by about 2,947 votes (50.2%) in the May 19 GOP primary. Martin requested a recount; the [updated margin was 2,974](https://thecurrentga.org/2026/06/18/primaries-and-runoffs-are-over-but-one-psc-race-lingers/), with certification expected July 1.

**Residency challenge.** A DeKalb resident who had worked on Hubbard's earlier campaigns challenged Johnson's residency. An administrative law judge found Johnson's legal domicile is in District 3 even though he spends much of his time at a Cobb County home, and the [Secretary of State let him stay on the ballot](https://www.fox5atlanta.com/news/georgia-public-service-commission-candidates-4-candidates-ballot). Johnson called the challenge politically motivated.

**Candidates in their own words.**
- **Johnson** says he has "no regrets" about his tenure and "feels for" customers paying higher bills ([AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/04/care-about-your-georgia-power-bill-these-psc-races-could-have-a-big-impact/)). He argues large-load customers are paying for "a better grid, a stronger grid," and he declined an interview for a [fall data-center story](https://www.bryancountynews.com/news/georgia-psc-candidates-talk-data-centers-ahead-of-november-election/). His written statement said data-center development is a matter of local control and that ratepayers should bear "not one cent" of the cost of serving OpenAI. His [campaign site](https://friendsforfitz.com) lists four themes: protect ratepayers, energy dominance, "hold the line against radical policies," and keep Georgia business-friendly.
- **Hubbard** opposes new fossil-fuel capacity for data centers and wants collateral, minimum-payment and exit protections in their contracts ([Bryan County News](https://www.bryancountynews.com/news/georgia-psc-candidates-talk-data-centers-ahead-of-november-election/)). He also wants clean-energy requirements and a PSC that does its own planning. His [campaign site](https://peterforgeorgia.com/news) lists the AFL-CIO endorsement and a joint appearance with Democratic gubernatorial nominee Keisha Lance Bottoms. It also includes a release attacking the GOP PSC ticket, which is campaign material, not neutral reporting.

**Polls.**

Nearly one in five were undecided. The same poll had Bottoms 46.4% and Jackson 44.9% for governor.

## State School Superintendent

**The office.** The superintendent runs the Georgia Department of Education: [distributing funds to districts, implementing statewide policy and negotiating with lawmakers](https://www.ajc.com/education/2026/04/8-candidates-vie-to-be-georgias-next-schools-superintendent/). The office shares statewide leadership with the [State Board of Education](https://gadoe.org/state-board/). The board's seats are aligned with congressional districts, and the governor fills them ([example appointment](https://gov.georgia.gov/node/19591)).

The [2026 Georgia Early Literacy Act](https://www.wrdw.com/2026/03/31/georgia-senate-passes-sweeping-education-overhaul-bill/) (Senate 49–0) gives the State Board power to approve K-3 reading materials and administer grants for literacy coaches. It also adds first-grade promotion rules starting in 2027. House lawmakers [kept Woods out of decisions on that law](https://thecurrentga.org/2026/05/29/years-of-tension-with-lawmakers-led-up-to-runoff-for-superintendent-richard-woods/).

**Woods's record and the GOP fight.** Woods took office in 2015 ([GaDOE](https://gadoe.org/superintendent/)) and says he removed Common Core, added cursive and financial literacy, and reduced testing. Points of tension with lawmakers, per the [Georgia Recorder](https://thecurrentga.org/2026/05/29/years-of-tension-with-lawmakers-led-up-to-runoff-for-superintendent-richard-woods/):
- A 2023 text from a staffer asked an advocate to help kill a literacy bill.
- A 2024 dispute over reading screeners.
- His 2024 move to block AP African American Studies, which he reversed after criticism from both parties.

On reading results, only 30% of fourth graders were proficient on the 2024 NAEP. Woods cites a State Board-adopted measure showing 65% at grade level. In the primary, Conservatives for Strong Schools spent about $900,000 against him, and Speaker Jon Burns backed Fred "Bubba" Longgrear. Woods took [49.9% in the primary](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2026/06/17/richard-woods-wins-republican-nomination-georgia-state-school-superintendent/) and [won the runoff with 52%](https://www.ajc.com/education/2026/04/8-candidates-vie-to-be-georgias-next-schools-superintendent/).

**Powell.** Powell won the [Democratic primary outright with about 50%](https://41nbc.com/may-19th-2026-election-results) over Anton Anthony and Otha Thornton. She did especially well in rural counties. At an August [Georgia Partnership for Excellence in Education forum](https://capitol-beat.org/?p=18796):
- Powell called for expanding state-funded pre-K, giving rural districts recruitment incentives such as housing subsidies and child care, and more money for high-poverty schools.
- Woods pledged higher pay for veteran teachers, cited $38.5 million in the FY2027 budget for school buses, and defended his literacy-coach work.

Their campaign platforms are on [Woods's plan page](https://www.woodsforsuper.com/plan.html) and [Powell's site](https://www.drlydiapowell.com/).

## Commissioner of Agriculture

**The office.** The [Georgia Department of Agriculture](https://agr.georgia.gov/) regulates food, fuel, animals, plants, soil and pesticides.
- **Fuel:** Inspectors visit [every gas station, normally every 24–30 months](https://agr.georgia.gov/fuel-licensing-and-inspections), to confirm consumers get the fuel they pay for. A state lab tests motor fuels for purity and octane. The department does not regulate fuel prices.
- **Hemp:** The [Hemp Program](https://agr.georgia.gov/hemp-program), created by the 2019 Hemp Farming Act, licenses growers, processors, manufacturers, wholesalers and retailers of consumable hemp, plus testing labs. It cites SB 494 (effective Oct. 1, 2024) for its consumable-hemp rules.
- **Also:** food-safety inspections of retail and manufactured food, and the Georgia Grown marketing program.

**Harper.** Harper is a seventh-generation Irwin County farmer and former state senator (2012–2022), elected commissioner in 2022. He had no primary opponent. His [official bio](https://agr.ga.gov/Commissioner-Tyler-Harper) credits him with:
- consumable-hemp "guardrails"
- a ban on farmland purchases by "adversarial foreign nations, like China"
- the Georgia Farmland Conservation Fund
- restoring the department's law-enforcement status

Gov. Kemp [endorsed him in August](https://www.fox5atlanta.com/news/gov-kemp-endorses-incumbent-agriculture-commissioner). His [campaign issues](https://tylerharperga.com/issues/) center on farmer support, trade, food safety, disaster aid and rural broadband.

**Juhan-Arnold.** Juhan-Arnold is a Snellville nonprofit founder making her first run for office. She [beat Sedrick Rowe Jr. 62%–38%](https://41nbc.com/may-19th-2026-election-results) in the primary. One AJC passage calls it a "runoff," but it was a two-way primary. Her nine-pillar "[Grow Georgia](https://katherineforgeorgia.com/Agenda)" agenda includes regional food hubs, veterinary readiness zones, a disaster "fast lane," a hemp compliance desk, and published service standards. Her [priorities page](https://katherineforgeorgia.com/Priorities) lists gas-pump accuracy, fuel quality and scale inspections. Several topics are listed there by title only, without text, including "Data Centers, Water & Farmland" and "Hemp (Legal Agriculture)." Her [endorsements](https://katherineforgeorgia.com/Endorsements) include Georgia Equality PAC, Red Clay Democrats, North Georgia CAN, Young Democrats of Georgia, JOLPOL and D4 Women in Action.

## Commissioner of Insurance (also Safety Fire Commissioner)

**The office.** The [Office of Commissioner of Insurance and Safety Fire](https://oci.georgia.gov/) licenses and regulates insurers, investigates insurance fraud and inspects buildings to prevent fire. **King.** Gov. He entered the 2026 U.S. Senate GOP primary and then [withdrew on July 24, 2025](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2025/07/24/king-suspends-us-senate-challenge-against-ossoff-will-seek-re-election-insurance-commissioner/) to seek re-election, saying, "I got into the U.S. Senate race to beat Jon Ossoff, not distract from the mission." His [issues page](https://www.votejohnking.com/issues) claims:
- more than $100 million recovered for consumers
- more than $22 million in fines, including a record $5 million penalty
- support for lawsuit reform, while pressing insurers to deliver the rate cuts they promised
- the Georgia Access state exchange (insurers rose from four to ten)
- more than 500 arson arrests and tripled arson K-9 teams

**Waites.** Waites is a former three-term state representative and Atlanta at-large council member who resigned from the council in March 2024. She [beat DeAndre Mathis about 58%–41%](https://cobbcountycourier.com/2026/06/georgias-runoffs-are-over-here-are-the-candidates-who-are-advancing-to-novembers-election/) in the June runoff after taking 42% in a five-way primary. Her [priorities](https://www.keishawaites.org/our-priorities) are a "Rate Freeze Initiative," ending ZIP-code and credit-score pricing, and a "Consumer Fraud Strike Force." In the runoff she said she would expand denied-claims investigations and operate within Kemp's tort-reform jury-award limits ([AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/06/insurance-commissioner-candidates-make-their-pitch-to-democrats-in-runoff/)).

**Waites ethics case.** The [AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/06/insurance-commissioner-candidates-make-their-pitch-to-democrats-in-runoff/) also reported:

# Constitutional amendments

Three proposed amendments to the Georgia Constitution are on the Nov 3, 2026 ballot. Each passed both chambers of the General Assembly by at least two-thirds, and each needs a simple majority of voters to be ratified. The numbering and texts below follow the official booklet of summaries prepared by Attorney General Chris Carr, Secretary of State Brad Raffensperger and Legislative Counsel Betsy Howerton: Amendment 1 = HR 32, Amendment 2 = HR 251, Amendment 3 = HR 1243. The Secretary of State's own copy of the PDF ([sos.ga.gov](https://sos.ga.gov/sites/default/files/2026-09/2026%20Constitutional%20Summaries%20booklet%20FINAL.pdf)) is behind a bot-check that blocked automated download, so I used the identical booklet as reposted by [Augusta-Richmond County](https://www.augustaga.gov/DocumentCenter/View/21852/2026-Constitutional-Summaries-booklet-FINAL), linked from the [Augusta Board of Elections notice](https://www.augustaga.gov/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/4245). Its ballot questions match the printed ballot word for word.

Some outlets and markets number the amendments differently. For example, Augusta's web notice lists the 9-1-1 fund first, and Kalshi labels the 9-1-1 measure "A2." Use the ballot order above.

Unless a resolution says otherwise, a ratified amendment takes effect on Jan 1 after the election under Art. X, Sec. I, Para. VI. The [governor's 2010 proclamation](https://sonnyperdue.georgia.gov/vgn/images/portal/cit_1210/30/19/166389168Const%20AMENDMENT%204%202010.pdf) shows how that rule is applied. None of the three resolutions sets a different date. The contingent parts of both companion statutes, HB 90 and HB 426 Section 4, take effect Jan 1, 2027.

## Amendment 1: Conservation-use acreage limit (HR 32)

**Ballot header:** "Increases the maximum qualifying acreage for assessment and taxation as bona fide conservation use property." (House Resolution 32, Ga. L. 2025, p. 1081)

**How conservation use (CUVA) affects a tax bill.** Most Georgia property is assessed at 40% of fair market value. Bona fide farm and timber land can instead be valued at its *current use*, that is, what it is worth as a farm or forest, rather than what a developer would pay ([Georgia DOR](https://dor.georgia.gov/property-tax-valuation)). The owner signs a 10-year covenant, and breaking it requires repaying the tax savings ([HR 32 text](https://www.legis.ga.gov/api/legislation/document/20252026/236234)). The AJC summarizes the effect as qualifying land being assessed at 40% of its current-use value instead of its full value ([AJC, Aug 10, 2026](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/08/here-are-state-constitutional-amendments-georgians-will-vote-on-in-november/)). Today one owner can enroll at most 2,000 acres. About 14.2 million acres were in CUVA in 2023 ([Georgia Forestry Association brief](https://gfagrow.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025GFA_Issue-Brief_Tax_wQTP.pdf)).

**What changes.** HR 32 changes one number in Art. VII, Sec. I, Para. III(e)(1): 2,000 becomes 4,000 acres "of a single owner." The companion statute, [HB 90](https://www.legis.ga.gov/api/legislation/document/20252026/236233) (Act 78), makes the same change throughout O.C.G.A. 48-5-7.4. That covers the per-owner limit, the allocation rules for family-owned farm entities, the category for environmentally sensitive land, the cap on multiple covenants, and the DOR statewide registry that enforces the limit. HB 90 takes effect Jan 1, 2027 only if voters ratify; otherwise it "shall stand repealed."

**Who benefits.** The AJC identifies the beneficiaries as owners of farms and timberland with more than 2,000 acres ([AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/08/here-are-state-constitutional-amendments-georgians-will-vote-on-in-november/)). According to the Georgia Forestry Association, CUVA is open to individual landowners and some family farms, which must be family partnerships. Large forest tracts of 200+ acres, including those held by businesses, can instead use the separate Forest Land Protection Act, which has no stated maximum ([GFA brief](https://gfagrow.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025GFA_Issue-Brief_Tax_wQTP.pdf); [DOR FLPA page](https://dor.georgia.gov/georgia-forest-land-protection-act)).

**Effect on local tax digests and schools.** I found no fiscal note or state estimate of the revenue effect. Property tax is levied by counties, cities and school districts. Coverage notes these concerns:
- The AJC reports reservations that approval could reduce property-tax revenue for local governments and schools, or extend an unneeded break to corporate farms ([AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/08/here-are-state-constitutional-amendments-georgians-will-vote-on-in-november/)).
- Capitol Beat reported that Georgia Conservancy president Katherine Moore told the 2024 Senate study committee that counties could push back over land leaving tax digests. The committee's report acknowledged a tradeoff between protecting farm economies and limiting future local tax growth. The article notes that Florida reimburses its local governments for forgone taxes ([Capitol Beat, Mar 18, 2025](https://capitol-beat.org/2025/03/voters-will-be-asked-next-year-whether-to-expand-conservation-tax-breaks-for-farmers/)).
- The Georgia Conservancy itself supported HB 90 and HR 32 ([2025 recap](https://georgiaconservancy.org/legislative-recap-2025/)).

**Organized opposition.** I found none. The Georgia Municipal Association lists itself as **neutral** on [HR 32](https://billtracker.gacities.com/legislation/69433) and [HB 90](https://billtracker.gacities.com/legislation/69429). I found no published position from ACCG (counties) or the Georgia School Boards Association.

**Legislative record** ([HR 32](https://www.legis.ga.gov/legislation/69433), [HB 90](https://www.legis.ga.gov/legislation/69429)):
- HR 32 passed the House 168–5 on Mar 3, 2025. All five no votes were Democrats: Reps. Park Cannon (HD 58), Viola Davis (HD 87), Derrick Jackson (HD 68), Ruwa Romman (HD 97) and Gabriel Sanchez (HD 42). Republicans voted 99–0.
- HR 32 passed the Senate 50–1 on Mar 18, 2025. The only no vote was Sen. Colton Moore (R).
- HB 90 passed the House 111–62 on Feb 20, 2025. All 62 no votes were Democrats; Democrats split 13 yes, 62 no.
- HB 90 passed the Senate 47–3.
- No stated reasons for the no votes were found.
- The Senate's parallel resolution, SR 56 by Sen. Sam Watson, passed the Senate 51–1 in Feb 2025 ([Capitol Beat](https://capitol-beat.org/?p=14200)). That is why some outlets report "51–1."
- The idea came from the 2024 Senate Study Committee on the Preservation of Georgia's Farmlands. That committee cited the loss of about 2.6 million acres of farmland over five decades ([GPB/Capitol Beat, Dec 2, 2024](https://www.gpb.org/news/2024/12/02/lawmakers-recommend-multi-faceted-approach-preserving-georgia-farmland)).

## Amendment 2: Nonpartisan probate judge elections (HR 251)

**Ballot header:** "Provides for the nonpartisan election of all probate court judges." (House Resolution 251, Ga. L. 2026, p. 1110)

**What probate judges do.** Probate courts handle wills and estates, appoint guardians and conservators, and issue marriage licenses and weapons-carry licenses. Some also hear traffic cases and misdemeanors ([AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/08/here-are-state-constitutional-amendments-georgians-will-vote-on-in-november/)).

**Who is partisan now, and why.** Georgia has about 1,600 judges, and nearly all run without party labels. The exception is **53 of the 159 county probate judges**, who still run on party tickets. Most counties made their probate races nonpartisan through local legislation ([Georgia Recorder via GPB, Sep 23, 2026](https://gpb.org/news/2026/09/23/ballot-question-in-november-would-make-georgias-probate-judges-nonpartisan)). The split comes from Art. VI, Sec. VII, Para. I(a):
- The Constitution makes superior, state and appellate judges nonpartisan.
- All other judges "continue to be selected in the manner … they were selected on June 30, 1983, until otherwise provided by local law" ([HR 251 text](https://www.legis.ga.gov/api/legislation/document/20252026/248549)).
- So each county's probate race stays partisan unless the General Assembly passes a local act for that county. An example is [HB 593 (2025)](https://www.legis.ga.gov/legislation/70714) for Baker County.

I did not find a published list of the 53 partisan counties. **Fulton County already elects its probate judge on the nonpartisan ballot.** Judge Kenya Johnson qualified as "Non-partisan" in 2024 ([Fulton County qualified-candidate list](https://fultoncountyga.gov/-/media/May-21-Election-Qualified-Candidates/2024-QUALIFIED-CANDIDATES-updated5224.pdf)). Passage would not change how Fulton's probate race appears on the ballot.

**Companion law.** [HB 426 (2025)](https://www.legis.ga.gov/api/legislation/document/20252026/237012) made magistrate elections nonpartisan from July 1, 2025. It also contains a conforming change to O.C.G.A. 21-2-139 that takes effect Jan 1, 2027 only if this amendment is ratified. HB 426 passed the House 111–59 and the Senate 43–11 ([bill page](https://www.legis.ga.gov/legislation/70275)).

**Arguments for:**
- Sponsor Rep. Kimberly New (R-Villa Rica) says "judges should serve the law, not a political party" ([AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/08/here-are-state-constitutional-amendments-georgians-will-vote-on-in-november/)).
- More than 60 leaders of both parties signed an open letter, including former Govs. Roy Barnes (D) and Nathan Deal (R). It argues that judges "should answer to the law and the Constitution."
- Chief Justice Nels Peterson told the legislature in February that requiring judges to align with a party "can only undermine" the courts.
- Some probate judges say party ties can lead litigants to doubt their rulings ([GPB/Georgia Recorder](https://gpb.org/news/2026/09/23/ballot-question-in-november-would-make-georgias-probate-judges-nonpartisan)).
- Rough Draft Atlanta counts nearly 70 signers, including former DeKalb CEO Michael Thurmond and the mayors of Augusta, Columbus, Albany and Athens ([Rough Draft Atlanta](https://roughdraftatlanta.com/2026/09/23/georgia-probate-judge-election-amendment/)). Note that article's headline wrongly describes the measure as moving judges "from appointments to ballot." Probate judges are already elected.

**Arguments against:**
- Rep. Charlice Byrd (R-Woodstock) voted no. She says party labels give voters information: "we need to know as individuals where you stand on issues."
- Byrd also says the 53 remaining counties can go nonpartisan through local acts if they choose.
- The Recorder notes that party labels can help down-ballot candidates in one-party counties, where voters often know little else about them.
- The association that lobbies for county governments is neutral ([GPB/Georgia Recorder](https://gpb.org/news/2026/09/23/ballot-question-in-november-would-make-georgias-probate-judges-nonpartisan)).

**Legislative record** ([HR 251](https://www.legis.ga.gov/legislation/70279)):
- The House adopted it 147–20 on Feb 11, 2026. Republicans voted 85–7 and Democrats 62–13.
- On Mar 19 the Senate vote was 31–18, short of the 38 votes needed. All 18 no votes were Democrats.
- The Senate reconsidered and adopted it 46–0 on Mar 31.

## Amendment 3: Next Generation 9-1-1 Fund (HR 1243)

**Ballot header:** "Authorizes creation of Georgia Next Generation 9-1-1 Fund for 9-1-1 systems expansion, maintenance, and operation." (House Resolution 1243, Ga. L. 2026, p. 1109)

**How 9-1-1 fees work now.** Local governments choose by resolution to impose two fees ([House Study Committee on Funding for NG911, final report](https://www.legis.ga.gov/api/document/docs/default-source/house-study-committee-document-library-page/2025/next-gen-911/funding-for-ng911-house-study-committee-final-report.pdf?sfvrsn=d55b4c7_4)):
- A **$1.50 monthly charge** on wireline, wireless and VoIP bills.
- A **$1.50 charge per prepaid wireless purchase**.

The Department of Revenue collects both fees. It keeps 0.25%, sends 0.75% to the Peace Officers' Annuity and Benefit Fund and 1% to the Georgia Emergency Communications Authority (GECA), and passes the rest to local governments:
- Monthly-bill fees are allocated by enhanced ZIP code.
- Prepaid fees are allocated by population.

Local governments must deposit the money in an Emergency Telephone System Fund restricted to 9-1-1 uses. State law also bars the state from appropriating the fees. The fees bring in about **$250 million a year** and cover about **55%** of 9-1-1 center budgets; local general funds cover the rest. Salaries and benefits make up about 71% of spending. As of May 2024, the report counts Georgia among eight states that had made no statewide progress toward NG911. HB 423 (2025) now sets the framework for that move.

**What changes.** HR 1243 adds Art. III, Sec. IX, Para. VI(s) ([text](https://www.legis.ga.gov/api/legislation/document/20252026/248550)). The General Assembly may create the fund by general law and direct revenue "from specified sources" into it. Spending is limited to 9-1-1 expansion, maintenance, operation and the transition to NG911, and "any other appropriation from the … Fund shall be void." Deposits "shall not lapse" and are exempt from the Constitution's general limits on dedicating revenue.

The amendment does not itself create the fund, change the fee or move any money. Future laws would decide the fund's sources and management, and approval "would not immediately change the 911 fee" ([AJC, with Aug 19 clarification](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/08/here-are-state-constitutional-amendments-georgians-will-vote-on-in-november/)). Capitol Beat describes the intent as moving 9-1-1 fees into one statewide fund, with the fee held at $1.50 ([Capitol Beat, Mar 31, 2026](https://capitol-beat.org/2026/03/georgia-lawmakers-put-911-upgrades-on-this-years-ballot/)).

**What the study committee recommended** ([final report](https://www.legis.ga.gov/api/document/docs/default-source/house-study-committee-document-library-page/2025/next-gen-911/funding-for-ng911-house-study-committee-final-report.pdf?sfvrsn=d55b4c7_4)). The 2025 House committee, chaired by Rep. Chuck Martin, recommended:
- Turning the local fees into a state fee, set no higher than the current $1.50, collected into a dedicated trust fund through a constitutional amendment.
- Starting the new system around FY2028.
- Replacing ZIP-code distribution with a formula based on readiness and services.
- Preventing diversion of the funds.
- Providing for retirement funding that now relies on 9-1-1 fees.

A state GIS cost study estimated NG911 data build-out and maintenance at **$52.5 million over six years**. Fulton, Cobb and Cherokee counties have already upgraded with local money, and smaller counties say they cannot afford the infrastructure ([Atlanta News First, Aug 27, 2025](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2025/08/27/georgia-lawmakers-search-funding-ideas-911-system-upgrades/)). A separate 2025 law, [HB 423](https://www.legis.ga.gov/legislation/70268), updated GECA's NG911 duties and requires new 9-1-1 systems to meet NG911 or wireless enhanced 9-1-1 standards.

**What "nonlapsing" means.** Normally, appropriated state money that is unspent and not under contract "lapse[s] and cease[s] to be available at the expiration of the general appropriations Act for that fiscal year" under Art. III, Sec. IX, Para. IV(c) ([Attorney General Opinion 93-9](https://law.georgia.gov/node/2376)). A nonlapsing fund keeps its unspent balance from year to year.

**History of diversion.**
- The Dec 2013 FCC report to Congress listed Georgia among states reporting the use of 9-1-1 fees for general-fund or other purposes in the reports issued in 2010, 2011 (by correction) and 2012.
- It also quotes a GAO finding that in 2011, "[s]ix states – Arizona, Georgia, Illinois, Maine, New York and Rhode Island" used almost $77 million of 9-1-1 funds for other purposes ([FCC report](https://transition.fcc.gov/pshs/911/Net%20911/NET_911_Act_Report_to_Congress_123113.pdf)).
- The 2025 study committee says Georgia "is not currently listed as a diverter" but has been placed on the FCC list several times since 2010.
- In 2017 the AJC reported that almost $5 million in prepaid 9-1-1 fees never reached local agencies. The Revenue commissioner attributed the shortfall to a refund to a company that had overpaid ([AJC via GovTech](https://www.govtech.com/budget-finance/Millions-in-Georgia-911-Funds-Missing.html)).

**Arguments for:**
- Rep. Martin says NG911 would let responders see video before they arrive and "makes the first responders safer."
- Senate Minority Leader Harold Jones II (D-Augusta) says some 911 callers today get no answer ([Capitol Beat](https://capitol-beat.org/2026/03/georgia-lawmakers-put-911-upgrades-on-this-years-ballot/)).
- The Georgia Municipal Association lists its position as **Support** ([GMA tracker](https://billtracker.gacities.com/legislation/72891)).

**Arguments against:** I found no organized opposition. The only legislative no votes came in a Senate floor dispute, described below. A Stephens County 911 director told Atlanta News First NG911 is "great in theory, but we will never be able to afford it." That is a comment on cost, not on the amendment ([ANF](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2025/08/27/georgia-lawmakers-search-funding-ideas-911-system-upgrades/)).

**Legislative record** ([HR 1243](https://www.legis.ga.gov/legislation/72891)):
- The House adopted it 162–0 on Mar 4, 2026.
- On Mar 19 the Senate vote was 31–14, short of two-thirds. All 14 no votes were Democrats, who said they had not been consulted and that policy was not the issue. Sen. Derek Mallow said: "I absolutely support law enforcement. I support 911" ([Center Square via The Augusta Press](https://theaugustapress.com/georgians-will-consider-three-constitutional-amendments-in-november/)).
- After reconsideration, the Senate adopted it 49–0 on Mar 31.

# Special election: school sales tax

On the back of the ballot. Researched October 14, 2026.

## School sales tax (E-SPLOST)

**What it is.** Every Fulton County voter sees the same question: whether to continue the 1% sales and use tax for education that Fulton has levied since 1997. The tax pays for capital costs such as buildings, technology, buses and security for two districts, Fulton County Schools (FCS) and Atlanta Public Schools (APS). By law it cannot pay salaries or other operating costs ([FCS FAQ](https://www.fultonschools.org/our-district/our-district/capital-plan-2032/faqs); [APS E-SPLOST page](https://www.atlantapublicschools.us/about/departments/operations/facilities/esplost)).

A Yes vote continues the existing penny. It does not add a new one. Collections would start July 1, 2027, when the current tax ends, and run up to 20 quarters (five years). Total collections are capped at $2,095,680,577: up to $1,306,195,350 for FCS projects and up to $789,485,227 for APS projects located in Fulton County. A Yes vote also authorizes up to $350 million in APS general obligation bonds ([concurrent resolutions and Notice of Election, FCS board packet](https://simbli.eboardsolutions.com/Meetings/Attachment.aspx?S=36031609&AID=2030725&MID=137179)). Georgia DOR's rate chart effective Oct. 1, 2026 lists combined sales tax rates of 7.75% for Fulton outside Atlanta, Hapeville, College Park and East Point; 8.75% in those three cities; and 8.9% in Atlanta. Each rate includes the 1% education tax ([DOR general rate chart](https://dor.georgia.gov/document/document/httpsdorgeorgiagovdocumentdocumentgeneral-rate-chart-effective-october-1-2026/download), listed on [DOR's sales tax rates page](https://dor.georgia.gov/sales-tax-rates-general)).

**Who put it on the ballot.** Georgia law requires the county school board and each independent school board in the county to adopt concurrent resolutions. The resolutions cite Ga. Const. Art. VIII, Sec. VI, Para. IV and Article 3 of Chapter 8 of Title 48 of the O.C.G.A. ([resolution](https://simbli.eboardsolutions.com/Meetings/Attachment.aspx?S=36031609&AID=2030725&MID=137179)). The Fulton County Board of Education approved its resolution on June 18, 2026 ([FCS board materials](https://www.fultonschools.org/our-district/our-district/capital-plan-2032/board-materials); [Board Bulletin 6/22/2026](https://news.fultonschools.org/details/~board/board-bulletin/post/board-bulletin-for-6222026)). FCS says the vote was unanimous ([FCS FAQ](https://www.fultonschools.org/our-district/our-district/capital-plan-2032/faqs)). The Atlanta Board of Education adopted the matching resolution, but this research could not confirm its vote date or tally. In December 2025, Fox 5 reported that the APS board was scheduled to vote in June 2026 on placing the measure on the ballot ([Fox 5](https://www.fox5atlanta.com/news/atlanta-public-schools-aps-forward-closures-repurposing-vote)). The resolutions direct the Fulton County Board of Registration and Elections to publish the call and the Notice of Election. The county's posted call, a file dated July 23, 2026, returned "not found" when checked on Oct. 14. Its text survives as Exhibit A of the resolution.

**The split.** Proceeds are divided between FCS and APS by the latest full-time-equivalent (FTE) enrollment count before the referendum ([resolution](https://simbli.eboardsolutions.com/Meetings/Attachment.aspx?S=36031609&AID=2030725&MID=137179)). APS's website says APS receives about 34% of Fulton collections and FCS about 66% ([APS](https://www.atlantapublicschools.us/about/departments/operations/facilities/esplost)). The 2021 ballot caps divided along the same lines: $1,213,397,340 for FCS and $618,145,815 for APS, out of $1,831,543,155 ([2021 call for election](https://www.cityofsouthfultonga.gov/DocumentCenter/View/5401/ESPLOST---Call-for-Election---1122021)). The 2026 caps work out to about 62% FCS and 38% APS. No source found explains the change. The 2026 total cap is about 14% above the 2021 cap.

**Fulton County Schools' plan (Capital Plan 2032).** The plan went to the board for action on June 11, 2026 ([board materials](https://www.fultonschools.org/our-district/our-district/capital-plan-2032/board-materials)). It totals about $1.319 billion, of which $1.306 billion is projected tax revenue, and the district says it does not rely on additional borrowing ([Board Bulletin 6/12/2026](https://news.fultonschools.org/details/~board/board-bulletin/post/board-bulletin-for-6122026)). Projected spending ([overview PDF](https://www.fultonschools.org/fs/resource-manager/view/a4ea2ef1-62ef-4b23-b174-eb94a4d20cce)):
- facilities master plan: $685.9 million
- technology: $300 million
- textbooks: $65 million
- transportation: $30 million
- furniture and equipment: $25 million
- safety and security: $15 million
- reserves and operating expenses: $197.6 million

The plan calls for two replacement elementary schools, one in Planning Region A (south of Atlanta) and one in Region C (north of the Chattahoochee). Aging, under-enrolled schools in those regions "will be evaluated for possible closure or consolidation" into them. The resolution also names 16 schools for critical-system work, including Centennial, Johns Creek, Langston Hughes, Milton, Tri-Cities and Westlake high schools. The board's plan schedules that work in waves from 2028 to 2032, along with track, turf, stadium, security-vestibule and LED projects ([full plan deck, June 2026](https://simbli.eboardsolutions.com/Meetings/Attachment.aspx?S=36031609&AID=2030095&MID=136586)). The same deck puts current and forecast facility needs for 2025-2035 at about $2.1 billion.

**Atlanta Public Schools' plan (E-SPLOST VII).** The resolution names five APS projects:
- Booker T. Washington High
- a School of the Arts on the G.W. Carver campus
- a new elementary school at the A.D. Williams site, or elsewhere in the Douglass feeder pattern
- Maynard Jackson High
- Midtown High

APS's sample list adds an addition at Sarah Smith Elementary, the Harper-Archer planetarium, stadium work at Therrell and North Atlanta high schools, and the Hollis pool. It also lists system replacements at 13 schools ranked by facility condition score, plus districtwide technology, security, buses, kitchen equipment, textbooks, arts and athletics ([APS](https://www.atlantapublicschools.us/about/departments/operations/facilities/esplost)). APS staff forecast about $831 million over five years and are budgeting for 90% of it ([Center for Civic Innovation, Sept. 2026](https://civicatlanta.org/blog/2026-09-06-sales-tax-renewal-for-schools-goes-before-voters-in-november)). Decaturish reports that this total combines about $789.5 million from Fulton's tax and $41.5 million from DeKalb's separate ESPLOST VII, which is on DeKalb ballots for the part of Atlanta in that county ([Decaturish](https://www.decaturish.com/voter_guide/what-esplost-projects-are-on-the-ballot-for-dekalb-voters/article_8bcb0406-5428-43c3-befe-e213251377be.html)). APS has published no per-project cost estimates that this research could find. FCS, by contrast, publishes category totals.

**The $350 million APS bond.** If the tax passes, APS may issue up to $350 million in general obligation debt, at no more than 6% interest. The debt would be repaid from APS's share of the tax, with maximum principal payments of $63.3 million in 2028 rising to $77.0 million in 2032. The board may borrow less. If APS's tax share ever falls short, the resolution directs Fulton commissioners to levy a property tax within Atlanta to cover the shortfall. The stated purpose is to build the APS projects "as soon as possible" ([resolution](https://simbli.eboardsolutions.com/Meetings/Attachment.aspx?S=36031609&AID=2030725&MID=137179)). APS staff told the board that bonds issued before collections arrive would let six projects start construction in 2027 ([CCI](https://civicatlanta.org/blog/2026-09-06-sales-tax-renewal-for-schools-goes-before-voters-in-november)):
- the A.D. Williams elementary
- additions at Midtown and Maynard Jackson high schools
- renovations at Washington High and the Carver arts school
- a retrofit of the APS Hub for a medical academy

**If it fails.** The current tax ends June 30, 2027 ([FCS FAQ](https://www.fultonschools.org/our-district/our-district/capital-plan-2032/faqs)). FCS says the other ways to fund capital projects are general obligation bonds, which need voter approval and are repaid with interest, or a higher maintenance-and-operations millage rate ([FCS FAQ](https://www.fultonschools.org/our-district/our-district/capital-plan-2032/faqs); [FCS funding options](https://www.fultonschools.org/fs/pages/41555)). A May 2026 FCS staff comparison put numbers on these options, assuming a $400,000 home ([FCS financing presentation, May 5, 2026](https://simbli.eboardsolutions.com/Meetings/Attachment.aspx?S=36031609&AID=2013141&MID=136052)):
- a 5-mill levy increase: about $800 a year
- a 20-year, $1.27 billion bond: 2 mills (about $320 a year) and $767 million in interest

That presentation compares options. It does not say what the board would choose. For APS, the Center for Civic Innovation reported that several APS Forward 2040 recommendations are "conditioned upon APS receiving additional funds through E-SPLOST." These include closing Usher-Collier and Scott elementaries for repurposing and moving their students to the new A.D. Williams school ([CCI, Oct. 2025](https://civicatlanta.org/blog/aps-facilities-recommendations-2025)). Fox 5 reported that "all the additions would require an ESPLOST" ([Fox 5, Oct. 2025](https://www.fox5atlanta.com/news/atlanta-public-schools-unveils-final-plan-repurpose-16-schools-save-millions)).

**Supporters and opponents.** Both school boards called the vote. FCS argues that the sales tax is "pay-as-you-go" with no interest and spreads costs to non-property owners, including people who come to Fulton to work, shop or eat. It credits the tax with retiring its long-term debt in January 2021 ([FCS FAQ](https://www.fultonschools.org/our-district/our-district/capital-plan-2032/faqs)). APS says the tax preserves operating funds for teaching and reduces reliance on property taxes and borrowing ([APS](https://www.atlantapublicschools.us/about/departments/operations/facilities/esplost)). As of Oct. 14, this research found no organized campaign for or against the measure, and no PTA or business-group endorsements. APS's 2025 school-closure plan drew opposition at public meetings ([Fox 5](https://www.fox5atlanta.com/news/atlanta-public-schools-aps-forward-closures-repurposing-vote)), but no source found ties that opposition to this vote. One general critique of sales taxes comes from coverage of a separate Athens tax vote. There, UGA professor Alex Combs said low-income households pay a higher share of their income in sales taxes ([Flagpole](https://flagpole.com/election2026/2026/10/07/how-a-november-flost-referendum-will-affect-athens-voters-taxes/)).

**Past results.** The November 2021 renewal passed with 105,773 Yes votes to 44,903 No votes, 70.2% to 29.8%, according to Fulton's official summary report ([copy hosted by UF Election Lab](https://election.lab.ufl.edu/data-downloads/localelections/GA/Alpharetta%2C%20City%20of%20%5BFulton%20County%5D/Election_Summary_Report%20%2866%29.pdf)). Election-night totals were nearly the same ([Rough Draft Atlanta](https://roughdraftatlanta.com/2021/11/03/fulton-voters-ok-education-transportation-sales-tax-extensions/)). Appen Media reported that the May 2016 renewal drew "just under 80 percent" ([Appen Media](https://www.appenmedia.com/schools/voters-approve-1-percent-sales-tax-for-fulton-county-schools/article_31a46a92-3c67-11ec-95d8-67f444fa53de.html)). Voters have approved E-SPLOST at each five-year vote since 1997, and APS lists E-SPLOST I through VI ([APS](https://www.atlantapublicschools.us/about/departments/operations/facilities/esplost)). No polling was found, and no sourced statewide E-SPLOST pass rate was found.

**Ballot text note.** The question text matches Exhibit A (the Notice of Election) of the concurrent resolutions. Fulton's consolidated sample ballots posted Sept. 28 and Oct. 8 show a stray "10" in the APS paragraph ("new 10 and replacement schools") that is not in the resolution ([sample ballot, Oct. 8](https://www.fultoncountyga.gov/-/media/2026-Election/November-3-Election/Sample-Ballot--Consolidated--Nov-32026-General--Special-Election--1082026.pdf)). The sample ballot's heading is "Educational Special Purpose Local Option Sales and Use Tax (ESPLOST)." The printed absentee ballot instead heads it "Local Option Sales Tax for Educational Purposes" under "Special Election", and its text matches the resolution, without the stray "10".
